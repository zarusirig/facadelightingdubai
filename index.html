The figure is driven by building height, facade area, fixture grade, access method, and control complexity — a 40-storey Business Bay tower and a Dubai Hills villa are not comparable jobs. Most Dubai facade lighting projects require compliance with the Al Sa’fat Green Building Evaluation System and DEWA electrical standards, and many need a Dubai Civil Defence NOC where the scheme touches emergency or egress circuits. Buildings inside Emaar, Nakheel, DAMAC or Meraas communities also face master-developer design review. Facade Lighting Dubai maps every applicable requirement before design begins and prepares the submission documents, so approvals stay off your critical path.

A villa scheme in Dubai is usually designed, approved and installed within three to six weeks. A commercial tower runs two to six months, because containment and cabling follow the access equipment up the building and DEWA approval timing sits on the critical path. Facade Lighting Dubai issues a milestone timeline with your proposal after the free site assessment, so you know the approval, installation and commissioning dates before you commit.
