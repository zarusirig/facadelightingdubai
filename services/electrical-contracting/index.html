Electrical Contractor in Dubai for Facade and Architectural Lighting

Facade Lighting Dubai is the lighting division of UNQOOD ALNUJOOM Electrical Fitting Contracting L.L.C, a licensed electrical fitting contractor based in Naif, Deira. We deliver the power side of a lighting scheme — supply, cabling, containment, earthing, drivers and control panels — under the same contract as the design and installation, so nobody can point at anybody else when a circuit trips.

What Our Electrical Contracting Covers

Most facade lighting failures in Dubai are not fixture failures. They are electrical failures — undersized cable, a driver cooked in an unventilated riser, or an earthing arrangement that never got tested. These are the packages we deliver, and each one is scoped from measured site conditions rather than a standard detail.

Facade lighting power infrastructure

Sub-mains, distribution boards and final circuits sized for the actual connected load of the lighting scheme, with diversity and voltage drop calculated across the full run. On a tower this means a dedicated lighting sub-main rather than borrowed ways on a landlord board. See the power consumption guide for how connected load is calculated.

LED driver, transformer and control panel installation

Drivers positioned where they can be reached and cooled, not sealed into a facade cavity that reaches 60°C in July. Panel builds are documented with schedules and labelled to match the as-built drawing. Driver placement is the single most common cause of the early failures covered in our LED driver failure guide.

DEWA-compliant cabling, containment and earthing

Containment routed and fixed to survive facade access equipment and shamal-season loading, with power and lighting-control data properly segregated. Earthing and bonding are tested and certified, not assumed. Our DEWA electrical requirements guide sets out what the authority checks.

Lighting control system wiring

DMX512, DALI and DALI-2 topologies wired to the protocol’s actual rules — correct cable type, terminated runs, sensible universe and address planning. A control system that was wired as if it were mains is the reason most Dubai facades flicker. Compare the protocols in our DMX versus DALI comparison.

Emergency and egress lighting circuits

Where a facade scheme touches emergency or egress lighting, the circuit falls under Dubai Civil Defence scrutiny and needs its own supply, testing regime and NOC. We handle that separately from the decorative circuits. See the emergency egress lighting requirements.

Electrical retrofit and upgrade

Adding facade lighting to an occupied building usually means working with a board that has no spare capacity and containment that does not go where you need it. We survey what exists, confirm what it can carry, and set out the options honestly — including when the answer is a DEWA capacity upgrade. See retrofit versus new-build installation.

Why a Single Contractor Beats a Split Package

The most common procurement route in Dubai is to buy fixtures from a lighting supplier and hand the wiring to whichever electrical subcontractor is already on site. It looks cheaper on the tender. It rarely is.

Our Process

  1. Survey the existing supply. We check incoming supply, distribution board capacity, spare ways and the earthing arrangement before any lighting load is added.
  2. Calculate the connected load. Load, diversity and voltage drop are calculated across the full cable run, so conductor sizes come from figures rather than assumptions.
  3. Prepare the DEWA documentation. Single-line diagrams, load schedules and the submission itself, plus any Dubai Civil Defence NOC the lighting package triggers.
  4. Install containment and cabling. Fixed to the approved route, with power and control data segregated.
  5. Install drivers and panels. Positioned to be accessible and ventilated, rated for the ambient temperature at that specific location.
  6. Test and commission. Insulation resistance, continuity, polarity and earth loop impedance across every circuit, then control system commissioning and certificate issue.

What Drives the Cost in Dubai

We do not publish a rate card for electrical work, because four variables move the figure too far for a rate to mean anything. A villa with the board ten metres from the facade and a tower needing a new riser sub-main are not the same job.

  • Cable run length and route. The single largest driver. Long runs force larger conductors to hold voltage drop within limits.
  • Spare capacity in the existing board. If there is none, the cost of a sub-main or a DEWA capacity upgrade dominates everything else.
  • Access method. Rope access and BMU work on a tower costs multiples of scaffold work at ground level, and wind suspension days extend the programme.
  • Control complexity. A switched white scheme needs a fraction of the wiring and commissioning time of a pixel-mapped RGBW facade.

Our facade lighting cost guide sets out the published benchmarks for the lighting package, and a free site assessment converts those into a scoped figure for your building.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Facade Lighting Dubai a licensed electrical contractor in Dubai?

Yes. Facade Lighting Dubai operates as the lighting division of UNQOOD ALNUJOOM Electrical Fitting Contracting L.L.C, a licensed electrical fitting contracting company based in Naif, Deira. That means the power infrastructure behind a facade lighting scheme — distribution, cabling, containment, earthing, drivers and control panels — is delivered by the same company that designs and installs the lighting, rather than split across a lighting supplier and a separate electrical subcontractor.

What does an electrical contractor do on a facade lighting project in Dubai?

An electrical contractor on a Dubai facade lighting project sizes and installs everything between the building’s distribution board and the fixture. That covers load calculations, cable sizing against voltage drop, containment routing, earthing and bonding, LED driver and transformer positioning, control panel build, lighting control wiring for DMX or DALI, and the testing and certification DEWA requires before energisation. Getting this layer wrong is the most common cause of premature LED failure in Dubai.

Do I need DEWA approval for facade lighting electrical work?

Yes, in almost all cases. Any addition to a building’s connected load in Dubai requires DEWA notification, and facade lighting on a tower can add tens of kilowatts. Facade Lighting Dubai prepares the single-line diagrams, load schedules and submission documents as part of the electrical package, and coordinates a Dubai Civil Defence NOC where the scheme affects emergency or egress lighting circuits.

How much does electrical contracting for facade lighting cost in Dubai?

Electrical cost on a facade lighting project is driven by cable run length, containment type, access method, and whether the existing distribution board has spare capacity. A villa scheme with a nearby board is a small fraction of the project cost; a tower needing a new sub-main, riser containment and rope-access installation is a much larger share. Facade Lighting Dubai gives a scoped figure after a free site assessment rather than a rate card, because the variables move the number too far.

Can you work on an existing building or only new construction?

Both. Retrofit work in Dubai is often the larger part of the job, because existing buildings rarely have spare capacity or containment routed where facade fixtures need it. Facade Lighting Dubai surveys the existing supply first, confirms what the current board and riser can carry, then designs the addition around it. Where capacity is short, we set out the options — load shedding, a dedicated sub-main, or a DEWA capacity upgrade — with the cost of each.

Do you cover Abu Dhabi and the Northern Emirates?

Yes. Facade Lighting Dubai delivers electrical and lighting packages across Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman and the Northern Emirates, with our workshop and stores in Naif, Deira. Requirements change outside Dubai — Abu Dhabi projects fall under Estidama and ADDC rather than Al Sa’fat and DEWA — so we confirm the applicable authority set during the assessment.

Who issues the test certificates after installation?

We do. On completion, Facade Lighting Dubai carries out and documents insulation resistance, continuity, polarity and earth fault loop impedance testing across every new circuit, then issues the certificates alongside the as-built single-line diagram, panel schedules and the lighting control programme backup. That documentation set is what your facilities team, your consultant and the authority will each ask for, and it is what makes a warranty claim straightforward later.

How long does the electrical work take on a facade lighting project?

On a villa in Dubai the electrical package is usually two to three weeks inside a three to six week overall programme. On a commercial tower it runs alongside the facade installation over two to six months, because containment and cabling follow the access equipment up the building. The binding constraint is rarely labour — it is DEWA approval timing and access equipment availability, both of which we programme in from the start.
